About

AI that makes collaborative behavior interpretable and actionable.

My research sits at the intersection of computer science and the learning sciences. I develop human-centered AI systems that combine language, vision, behavioral signals, and interaction traces to characterize how people communicate, contribute, coordinate, and respond to one another.

A central theme of my work is moving from analytics to support: translating computational evidence into feedback that learners can inspect, question, and use to regulate future collaboration. I am also interested in the privacy and agency challenges created by multimodal sensing and AI-mediated analytics.

My broader research interests include human-centered AI, multimodal learning analytics, LLM-based feedback and agents, computer-supported collaborative learning, privacy-preserving multimodal sensing, and affective computing.

Research

Current research directions

My work connects computational modeling with the design and evaluation of systems that support reflection, regulation, collaboration, and learner agency.

01

Multimodal Learning Analytics

Modeling collaborative activity using discourse, participation, video, gaze, audio, temporal traces, and other behavioral signals.

02

LLM-Based Feedback & Agents

Designing evidence-grounded LLM systems and agentic analytics pipelines that reason over collaboration evidence and support reflective dialogue.

03

Collaboration Literacy

Developing indicators, frameworks, and behavioral profiles that help learners interpret collaboration quality and regulate future group work.

04

Privacy-Preserving Multimodal Sensing

Studying visual privacy, representation, transparency, and learner agency while retaining useful signals for multimodal analytics.

05

Affective Computing

Investigating computational approaches for understanding affective expression, masked emotion, and socio-emotional participation.

06

Human-Centered Evaluation

Combining randomized field studies, mixed-effects modeling, clustering, qualitative analysis, and systematic review methods.
